FAA Ground Stop Lifted After Communications Failure — Why Flights May Still Be Disrupted

A rare nationwide halt ended Friday evening, but airlines faced a difficult restart as officials examined aging air-traffic technology.

Published November 14, 2026 · Texas News Studio News Desk

What happened

The Federal Aviation Administration lifted its nationwide ground stop Friday evening after a major communications failure disrupted air traffic control operations across the United States. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said inconsistent radar and communications data led officials to suspend departures as a safety precaution. The order was lifted at approximately 8:15 p.m. Eastern, according to reports from Reuters and the Associated Press.

Why the disruption may continue

Restarting flights does not immediately put aircraft, flight crews and passengers back where they need to be. Airlines faced thousands of cancellations, with recovery expected to take time. Travelers should check their airline directly before leaving for the airport, even if the national ground stop has ended.

Was it a cyberattack?

Officials said they had not found evidence of a cyberattack. Duffy pointed to overloaded older switches and routers as a likely source of the failure, while investigators continued examining the sequence of events. The distinction matters: a serious technology failure can disrupt national infrastructure without evidence of hostile interference.

The larger question

The episode has renewed scrutiny of the aging air traffic control system and proposals to modernize it. The immediate ground stop is over, but the longer-term issue is how to prevent another communications failure from forcing a nationwide halt.
