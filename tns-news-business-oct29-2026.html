AI POWER BOOM: WILL AMERICAN FAMILIES PAY THE PRICE?

AI-generated illustrative image; not a documentary photograph.

America’s artificial-intelligence boom is fueling massive investments in data centers, computer hardware and electrical infrastructure. Supporters see jobs, innovation and a chance for the United States to lead the next technological era. But the surge is also raising an uncomfortable question: who pays for the power?

Data centers can generate construction work and tax revenue, yet they also require substantial electricity, water and transmission capacity. Communities are debating whether infrastructure costs might fall on ordinary utility customers. Those concerns deserve more than glossy promises from developers.

A pro-growth approach does not mean writing blank checks. Local officials should disclose incentives, explain utility arrangements and demand realistic forecasts. Businesses should compete and invest, while taxpayers should know exactly what they are being asked to subsidize.

The best outcome is American technological leadership without hidden costs for homeowners and small businesses. That starts with transparent deals and measurable results.
