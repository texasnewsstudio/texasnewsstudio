The Lesser Evil: Why Voters Sometimes Choose Against a Candidate

Every election season seems to revive the same uneasy argument: must voters settle for the lesser evil? The phrase is familiar, but the choices behind it are personal and often complicated.

Here is the situation

Many voters choose a candidate primarily to prevent an outcome they regard as worse. Others reject that calculation because they believe it rewards weak accountability or discourages alternatives. Both positions involve tradeoffs between immediate consequences and long-term political incentives.
