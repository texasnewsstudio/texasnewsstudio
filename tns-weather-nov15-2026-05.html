Coastal Thunderstorms: Why a Marginal Risk Still Matters

A marginal risk is level one on the Storm Prediction Center's five-level severe-thunderstorm scale. For the middle and lower Texas coast Sunday, that means isolated severe storms are possible, but widespread severe weather is not the expected outcome.

The 7 a.m. outlook describes strong wind shear and instability near the Corpus Christi area, ingredients that can help some thunderstorms intensify. The atmosphere is less favorable farther inland in some areas, which makes the location of individual storms important. Forecasters explicitly note that stronger storms could stay offshore.

Residents should not confuse a risk category with a warning. An outlook describes a broad possibility hours in advance; a severe thunderstorm warning identifies a more immediate threat in a specific area. A warning calls for prompt protective action even if the day's overall outlook was only marginal.

For now, the most useful preparation is straightforward: enable weather alerts, know where to shelter, and postpone water activities when lightning develops. Check updated SPC outlooks and local NWS warnings because the situation can evolve through the afternoon.
