Central Texas Watches Sunday's Storm Track as Front Moves East

A passing upper-level weather disturbance and advancing cold front are creating a changing forecast across Central Texas on Sunday. The question is not simply whether it rains, but where stronger thunderstorms organize as the system moves east and southeast.

The Storm Prediction Center's earlier outlook highlighted the possibility of isolated strong to severe thunderstorms across central and south Texas. Its Sunday morning update narrowed the principal marginal severe-weather risk toward the middle and lower coast. That shift matters: an older forecast map should not be presented as the latest risk for Austin or San Antonio.

For communities along the Interstate 35 corridor, showers and isolated thunderstorms remain a reason to follow local forecasts, but the coastal zone has the more clearly defined severe-weather risk in the latest national outlook. A thunderstorm can still bring lightning and brief heavy rain outside a categorical severe-risk area.

Sunday plans should account for changing conditions rather than assume the whole region will see identical weather. Check radar before longer drives, move indoors when thunder is heard and never cross flooded low-water crossings. Weather alerts and local NWS updates take priority over any morning summary.
