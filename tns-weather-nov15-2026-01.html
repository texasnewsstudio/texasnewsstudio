North Texas Sunday Soaker: Rain, Thunder and a November Chill

The National Weather Service in Fort Worth expects widespread rain Sunday, with precipitation chances of 80 to 100 percent. Temperatures are forecast to remain in the upper 40s and 50s, a sharp departure from the mild conditions Texans experienced earlier.

The setup involves an upper-level disturbance moving out of the southern High Plains. That disturbance supplies lift, while cooler air near the ground keeps Sunday feeling raw. Forecasters also allow for isolated thunderstorms, but the main story for most of North Texas is widespread rainfall rather than a regionwide severe-weather outbreak.

The timing matters for drivers: wet roads and reduced visibility can slow Sunday travel, especially where heavier showers develop. Residents should allow extra braking distance and avoid driving through water covering a road. A few rumbles of thunder do not automatically mean a severe storm, but any thunderstorm can produce dangerous lightning.

The colder weather is expected to persist into Monday. The forecast calls for temperatures to recover later in the week. The sensible takeaway is to plan for a damp, chilly Sunday and keep checking the latest forecast as bands of rain move through.
