THE ECONOMY AFTER THE MIDTERMS · NOVEMBER 8, 2026

VOTERS DEMAND RELIEF AS WASHINGTON FACES ECONOMIC TEST

After the midterms, both parties confront pressure over prices, deficits and household budgets.

The election is over, but the economic frustration that helped shape it is not. With Democrats taking control of Congress, both parties face new pressure to respond to voters worried about the price of everyday life.

Food, energy, housing and healthcare remain major household concerns. The political question is whether Washington can turn competing campaign promises into policies that meaningfully improve affordability.

The challenge is complicated by federal borrowing, interest costs and disagreements over taxes and spending. Economic experts caution that inflation cannot be brought down overnight through a single vote or executive decision.

The next Congress will also confront longer-term issues, including Social Security financing and the impact of artificial intelligence on jobs and productivity.

For Texas families, the test is practical: whether paychecks stretch farther and essential costs become easier to manage. TNS will focus on the policy decisions and measurable results, not just the political arguments.
