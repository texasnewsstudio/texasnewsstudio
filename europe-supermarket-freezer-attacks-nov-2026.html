Hackers Disrupt European Supermarket Freezers, Forcing Stores to Throw Away Food

Remote manipulation of refrigeration settings has caused costly food losses across multiple European countries.

Reporting note: Confirmed events and official warnings are distinguished from hypothetical scenarios and unverified attribution.

A wave of cyber incidents involving internet-connected refrigeration equipment disrupted supermarkets in several European countries in early November. Stores reported fridges and freezers running too warm, creating immediate problems for food safety and inventory.

The incidents followed a previous wave in France in October. Researchers have pointed to exposed controllers, weak configurations and older software as possible risk factors, while investigations into the specific intrusion methods continue.

The scale matters because a refrigeration controller is a small piece of equipment with a large real-world role. A single unsafe temperature setting can force a store to discard valuable perishable goods.

Authorities have not conclusively identified the attackers or established that every incident is linked. Claims blaming a particular country should be treated as unproven.
