DEL RIO ROADS FACE FLOODING AFTER OVERNIGHT DOWNPOURS

November 8, 2026 | Texas News Studio

The rain did not have to cover all of Texas to create a serious problem on one stretch of roadway. Early Sunday, November 8, the National Weather Service issued a flood advisory for southeastern Val Verde County, including Del Rio and nearby communities. That advisory was scheduled to run until 7 a.m. CST, and it was a reminder of how quickly a routine trip can become dangerous when water begins collecting in low spots.

At 4:14 a.m., forecasters reported that Doppler radar indicated heavy thunderstorms over the area. Up to one inch of rain had already fallen, and another one to two inches was expected in the advisory area. The agency warned of minor flooding in low-lying and poorly drained locations, including water over roadways. The bulletin named Del Rio, Lake View, Val Verde Park, Laughlin Air Force Base and several recreation areas near Amistad Reservoir among places that could be affected.

An advisory is not the same as a flash flood warning. It signals a lower level of anticipated impact, but it is not a declaration that every road is passable. The real danger often comes from the geography of a particular crossing: a creek that is normally shallow, a dip in the pavement or an underpass that collects runoff. Rain may be lighter at the driver's location than upstream, where the water actually originated.

For residents, the practical response is straightforward. Check current road conditions before traveling, especially near low-water crossings. Never use the depth of water as a guess about whether a road is safe, and do not assume the absence of rain overhead means a crossing is clear. Officials' advice remains to turn around rather than drive through flooded pavement.

This was an early-morning bulletin, not a claim that the advisory remains active throughout Sunday. Conditions and warning boundaries can change rapidly. TNS readers should consult the current NWS alert feed and local transportation authorities for any active restrictions before heading out. The bigger lesson from Del Rio is that localized flooding can matter just as much to the person on that road as a much larger storm elsewhere in the state.

Primary source: National Weather Service / NOAA. Historical advisories may have expired; check current alerts before travel.
