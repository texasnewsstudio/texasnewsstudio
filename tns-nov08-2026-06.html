EUROPE’S YOUNG TAKE TO THE STREETS

Housing costs, weak wage growth and dim job prospects fuel a widening wave of youth protests.

TNS News Desk | November 8, 2026

Across Europe, a growing number of young people are asking a question that should trouble their political leaders: What good is national prosperity if a generation cannot afford a place to live or see a path to a better life?

A November 8 report carried by The Liberty Daily and originally published by The Epoch Times described protests over housing, education, public services and the cost of living in several European countries. The grievances differ by city, but the shared concern is stalled opportunity.

The report cited Eurostat figures putting unemployment among people under 25 above 15 percent in both the eurozone and the European Union. That statistic alone does not explain every protest, but it provides important context for why younger workers feel exposed.

High rents and energy bills can consume paychecks even when people are employed. Meanwhile governments face pressure to finance pensions, health systems, defense and public debt, leaving difficult choices about spending priorities.

Not every protest has the same cause, and isolated violence should not be confused with the demands of all demonstrators. Still, the political warning is unmistakable: when an entire age group doubts that working hard will improve its future, frustration can become a lasting force in elections and public life.
