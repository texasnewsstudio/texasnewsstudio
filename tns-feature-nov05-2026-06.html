The Blue Wave and the Debate Over Whether Elections Change Policy

Election night always brings the familiar promises: a new direction, a fresh start, a political earthquake. But what actually changes once the cheering stops and the governing begins?

Here is the situation

Some commentators describe U.S. elections as a recurring contest that leaves major institutions and policy priorities intact. That is a political interpretation, not evidence that election results are predetermined or fraudulent. Elections can change legislative agendas, committee oversight, budgets and appointments, even while structural constraints remain.
