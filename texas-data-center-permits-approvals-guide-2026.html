Texas Data Center Permits: How to Track Approvals

A practical guide to understanding zoning, environmental permits and utility approvals before calling a project greenlit.

October 27, 2026 · Texas News Studio

AI-generated conceptual image; not a photograph of a specific facility.

When a company announces a Texas data center, that does not automatically mean construction is approved. Different decisions may come from a city, county, water district, utility or state agency.

Start with local planning and zoning agendas, then look for building permits and environmental filings where applicable. The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality provides information about certain air, water and stormwater authorizations.

Electric service is another separate hurdle. Large-load interconnection and transmission needs may affect a project’s schedule even if local land-use permissions are in place.

Readers should look for dated primary documents and identify exactly what was approved. A preliminary filing, an application under review and a final authorization are not interchangeable.

Editorial note: This background explainer does not report a newly approved project. Project-specific claims will require dated primary sources.
