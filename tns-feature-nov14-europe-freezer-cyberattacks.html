Hackers Target Supermarket Freezers Across Europe — A Warning for Connected Infrastructure

Refrigeration systems were manipulated remotely in several countries, forcing some stores to discard food.

Published November 14, 2026 · Texas News Studio News Desk

What investigators and retailers reported

A wave of cyber incidents affected refrigeration systems at supermarkets across parts of Europe on November 2. Reuters reported that stores in countries including Belgium and Sweden experienced unauthorized temperature changes that spoiled refrigerated or frozen goods.

Why refrigeration is vulnerable

Modern cooling equipment can be connected to remote monitoring and maintenance systems. That convenience creates a risk when access controls or exposed equipment are compromised: an attacker may be able to change operating settings without physically entering a store.

How broad was the incident?

Reports describe multiple chains and countries, but the precise number of affected locations and whether every incident shares the same perpetrator remain uncertain. Investigators have not publicly established a single responsible group.

The wider lesson

Cybersecurity is not only about stolen passwords or inaccessible websites. Connected physical systems can affect food safety, business continuity and supply chains. Retailers and equipment providers are reassessing how remote controls are protected.
