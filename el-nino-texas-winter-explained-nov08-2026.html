EL NIÑO IS HERE: WHAT IT CAN — AND CANNOT — MEAN FOR TEXAS WINTER

November 8, 2026 | Texas News Studio

Every time the phrase El Niño appears in a headline, Texans begin asking the same question: does that mean another severe winter is coming? The answer is more complicated than a single dramatic forecast graphic, and it begins with understanding what NOAA actually announced.

NOAA reported earlier in 2026 that El Niño conditions had formed and were expected to strengthen. El Niño describes unusually warm ocean waters in the central and eastern tropical Pacific and changes in the atmosphere above them. Those changes can influence the jet stream and shift the odds of wet, dry, warm or cool conditions in different parts of the country.

But the ocean does not control the weather in Texas like a thermostat. It changes the background probabilities. It does not identify the week a cold front will arrive, guarantee snow in Dallas or forecast ice on a particular highway in the Hill Country. Those shorter-range outcomes depend on the placement of weather systems, available moisture and temperatures close to the ground.

That is why it is possible to have a season influenced by El Niño and still experience long stretches of ordinary weather. The effect is best evaluated over many weeks, not by judging a single afternoon thunderstorm. A seasonal forecast can be useful for planning and still fail to describe the weather on any one date.

For Texas News Studio readers, the most useful approach is to follow two levels of information. First, use NOAA's climate outlooks for the broad pattern and the uncertainty surrounding it. Second, use National Weather Service forecasts and alerts when a specific cold front, rain event or winter-weather threat enters the reliable short-range forecast window.

There is a temptation to turn every winter climate headline into a prediction of disaster. We are not going to do that. El Niño is worth watching closely, especially for its influence on the southern United States, but the responsible story is about changing odds and preparing for whatever the actual forecasts eventually show.
