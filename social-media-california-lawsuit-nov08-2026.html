GOOGLE, META AND TIKTOK CHALLENGE CALIFORNIA CHILD-SAFETY LAW

A major court fight pits youth protections against technology companies’ free-speech claims.

Three of the world’s biggest technology companies have gone to court to challenge a California law restricting certain social-media features for children younger than sixteen.

Google, Meta and TikTok argue that the law improperly controls how their platforms select and present content, raising First Amendment and federal-law concerns. California officials say the measure is intended to protect children from features designed to encourage compulsive use.

The disputed provisions include personalized recommendations and autoplay. The law is scheduled to take effect in January 2027 unless the courts intervene.

The lawsuits put a larger national question into focus: how far states can go in regulating social-media products without infringing constitutional protections or placing new burdens on families and online speech.

No final court decision has resolved the challenge. TNS will track the litigation and its implications for parents, young users and the technology industry.
