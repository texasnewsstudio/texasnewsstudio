UK Cyber Chief Warns AI-Enabled Attacks Could Disrupt Critical Infrastructure by 2028

Britain's cybersecurity agency sees rising risk from AI-assisted intrusions, but says a future attack is not a confirmed current event.

Reporting note: Confirmed events and official warnings are distinguished from hypothetical scenarios and unverified attribution.

Britain's National Cyber Security Centre has warned that artificial intelligence could make existing cyberattack techniques faster and more scalable, increasing the pressure on organizations that operate essential services.

NCSC chief Richard Horne has identified the possibility of AI-enabled attacks affecting critical infrastructure within the next two years. The forecast concerns risks to organizations, including energy, transport and health services.

AI does not have to invent a new type of vulnerability to create problems. It can help criminals identify exposed systems, automate phishing or scale known techniques across many targets.

The warning should not be interpreted as confirmation that an AI-driven attack has already disabled Britain's power grid. The recent refrigeration incidents are real, but officials have not established them as AI-enabled attacks.
