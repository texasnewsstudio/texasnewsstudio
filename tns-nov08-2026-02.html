NEW ZEALAND ELECTION ON A KNIFE EDGE

A narrow parliamentary margin and uncounted special ballots leave the next government uncertain.

TNS News Desk | November 8, 2026

New Zealand went to the polls, but voters may have to wait weeks to know who will govern. Preliminary figures left Prime Minister Christopher Luxon’s existing coalition with only a slender advantage, while a significant block of ballots remained uncounted.

Reuters reported that National and its partners ACT and New Zealand First were projected to hold 67 seats in a 131-seat parliament. That is just above the 66-seat threshold for a majority. Roughly 14 percent of ballots were special votes, with the final count scheduled for November 27.

Special votes include ballots cast overseas and outside voters’ home electorates. Their eventual distribution could shift the parliamentary arithmetic. That means no responsible analysis can treat the initial seat projections as the final verdict.

The centrist Opportunity Party could become pivotal if the current coalition loses its majority. Luxon, who had previously ruled out working with the party, appeared more open to a possible relationship after the vote.

The broader story is economic discontent. Both major parties struggled to command strong popular support in an election dominated by living costs and a sluggish economy. Until the final count and coalition negotiations conclude, New Zealand faces a period of political uncertainty rather than a confirmed change of government.
