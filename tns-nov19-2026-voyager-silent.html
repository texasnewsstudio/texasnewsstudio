16 BILLION MILES AWAY — AND SUDDENLY SILENT! NASA'S VOYAGER HITS HISTORIC MILESTONE AS DATA VANISHES

The most distant spacecraft ever built has reached a staggering one-light-day distance from Earth. But at the very moment NASA prepares to celebrate, engineers are investigating a troubling communications mystery.

Texas News Studio branded alert graphic; not a photograph of Voyager 1.

Texas News Studio | November 19, 2026 — After nearly half a century in space, Voyager 1 has crossed a distance no human-made object has ever reached: roughly 16.1 billion miles from Earth. A radio signal now needs about 24 hours to travel from Earth to the spacecraft — and another 24 hours to return.

Yet NASA revealed Wednesday night that the legendary probe stopped sending science and engineering data after November 14. Ground antennas in Canberra, Australia, detected a carrier signal, but no accompanying data. Engineers are now trying to determine why.

Is Voyager 1 lost?

No. NASA says the continuing carrier signal indicates that Voyager 1 remains pointed toward Earth and its antenna and radio equipment are functioning. The problem may involve the onboard computer that gathers and formats data from the spacecraft's systems. NASA has not announced a definitive cause.

Mission specialists expect to confirm the one-light-day milestone on November 19 using the carrier signal and calculations of the spacecraft's trajectory. The milestone itself was expected on November 18.

A 48-hour conversation with the edge of interstellar space

Voyager 1 launched in 1977 and flew past Jupiter and Saturn before entering interstellar space in 2012. Now traveling at roughly 38,000 miles per hour, it carries a Golden Record containing sounds and images from Earth — a message intended for any distant civilization that might someday encounter it.

At this extraordinary distance, troubleshooting is slow. Any instruction takes a full day to arrive, and a reply takes another day to get home. NASA warns that diagnosing the present issue could take weeks or longer.

A mystery, not a confirmed mission failure

Voyager 1 previously experienced a data-transmission problem in 2023 and a temporary communications interruption in October 2026. NASA is investigating whether the latest problem is connected to the October event. The probe's longevity remains an extraordinary engineering achievement, even as its aging systems and shrinking power supply make continued operations increasingly difficult.

The bottom line: Voyager 1 has reached an astonishing new frontier, but NASA's engineers are working to restore the scientific information that makes its journey so valuable. The spacecraft is not confirmed lost.
