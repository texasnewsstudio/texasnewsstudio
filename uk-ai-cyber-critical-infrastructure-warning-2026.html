UK Cyber Chief Warns AI-Powered Attacks Could Hit Critical Infrastructure by 2028

Britain’s NCSC warns artificial intelligence could accelerate attacks on essential services over the next two years.

Important: This report discusses confirmed announcements, reported incidents and emergency-planning scenarios. It does not indicate that a nationwide blackout or cyberattack is imminent.

The UK National Cyber Security Centre warned on November 3 that AI-powered malicious software is highly likely to feature in a ransomware attack on British critical national infrastructure by 2028. This is an assessment of future risk, not a report that such an attack has occurred.

Critical infrastructure includes sectors such as energy, healthcare, transportation, communications and food supply. An intrusion can be disruptive even when the initial point of entry appears minor.

NCSC chief Richard Horne said outdated and poorly secured technology leaves organisations vulnerable as attackers use increasingly powerful tools to identify weaknesses.

The agency urged organisations to invest in resilience, address known vulnerabilities, and prepare for disruption rather than relying solely on preventing every intrusion.
