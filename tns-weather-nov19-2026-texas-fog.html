FOG SWALLOWS WEST TEXAS! DRIVERS WARNED AS VISIBILITY VANISHES

Dense morning fog threatens roads from the Permian Basin to mountain corridors.

Branded weather alert illustration, not a photograph of the event.

Texas News Studio Weather Desk | November 19, 2026

A thick blanket of fog developed across parts of West Texas and southeastern New Mexico Thursday morning, with the National Weather Service warning of hazardous driving conditions.

The Midland/Odessa forecast office identified the northwestern Permian Basin, Guadalupe and Delaware mountains, Van Horn and the Interstate 10 corridor, and parts of Big Bend as affected areas. The fog can sharply reduce visibility, particularly in valleys and low-lying locations.

The outlook calls for warmer and mainly dry weather through the end of the week, followed by a Sunday cold front and a slight chance of rain early next week. Conditions can change quickly, so travelers should check current advisories.

Weather forecasts and warnings can change. For urgent decisions, follow the latest National Weather Service advisories.
