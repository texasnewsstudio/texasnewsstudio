TEXAS POWER SHIFT: WHAT CHANGES AFTER ABBOTT DEFEAT?

Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa prepares to take office, but the Legislature will shape how much she can change.

Texas is preparing for a change of political leadership after Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated three-term Republican Governor Greg Abbott in the November 3 election. The result ends a long stretch of Republican control of the governor’s office and raises immediate questions about the state’s direction.

Hinojosa, an Austin legislator who made public education and household costs central campaign issues, has pledged to govern across party lines. Her victory does not mean every campaign proposal will become law. Texas has a Legislature, and its chambers can block or reshape major initiatives.

One immediate debate concerns the border. Abbott made Operation Lone Star a defining policy of his administration. Hinojosa criticized its costs, but the precise future of the operation remains undecided. Changing enforcement priorities would involve budget decisions, state agencies and coordination with federal authorities.

Education is another likely flashpoint. Hinojosa has opposed the private-school voucher program promoted by Abbott, while supporters argue that school choice expands opportunities for families. Those competing priorities will be tested when lawmakers return to Austin.

For Texans, the most useful questions are concrete: what happens to school funding, border spending, taxes and electricity costs? Texas News Studio will follow the decisions that affect residents rather than assume the election result answers them all.
