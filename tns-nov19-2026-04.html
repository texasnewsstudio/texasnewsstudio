THE WHALE WITH THE CROOKED FACE! 6-MILLION-YEAR-OLD SEA MONSTER REVEALED

A prehistoric whale's skull was so lopsided that researchers first suspected the fossil had been crushed.

Texas News Studio alert artwork — illustrative graphic, not a photograph of the discovery.

Texas News Studio | November 19, 2026

A bizarre extinct pygmy sperm whale from Peru had one of the most dramatically asymmetrical faces ever documented in a mammal, according to research published this week.

The species, Scaphokogia huachafo, lived around six million years ago. Its skull has a huge downward-curving snout and bony nostrils displaced to one side, features that initially made scientists wonder whether geological pressure had distorted the fossil.

Researchers found the unusual anatomy was genuine rather than the result of fossil damage. They believe its heavy snout, flexible head movement and specialized sound-producing structures may have helped it search for prey near the seafloor.

The animal was about 2.7 meters long, far smaller than today's giant sperm whales. Its strange appearance offers a window into an ancient marine ecosystem and a feeding strategy no longer seen in living whales.

The findings were reported by researchers including scientists at the University of Zurich in Communications Biology.
