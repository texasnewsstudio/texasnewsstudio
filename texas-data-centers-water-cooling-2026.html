How Much Water Do Texas Data Centers Need?

Cooling design, climate and local water supplies shape the environmental debate over new computing facilities.

October 27, 2026 · Texas News Studio

AI-generated conceptual image; not a photograph of a specific facility.

Servers generate heat, so every data center needs cooling. Some facilities use evaporative systems that consume water; others use air-cooled or closed-loop designs that may use less water but require different energy trade-offs.

There is no single water-use figure that applies to every Texas data center. Demand depends on equipment density, cooling technology, weather and how the site is operated.

In drought-prone areas, residents can ask developers to publish expected annual withdrawals, peak-day use, the water source and plans for reclaimed water.

Water-use claims deserve context: a project’s projected maximum, its permitted withdrawal and its actual consumption are three different numbers. Local water authorities and permit filings are key sources.

Editorial note: This background explainer does not report a newly approved project. Project-specific claims will require dated primary sources.
