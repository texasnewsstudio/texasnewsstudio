Texas Gulf Coast Faces Isolated Severe Storm Risk Sunday

The Storm Prediction Center's 7 a.m. Sunday outlook places the middle and lower Texas coast under a marginal severe-thunderstorm risk, the lowest of its five categorical risk levels. The concern is isolated stronger storms, not a forecast of widespread destructive weather.

Meteorologists are tracking an upper-level disturbance and a slow-moving front. Moisture and instability are more favorable near the Gulf than farther inland, and some storms may stay offshore. Still, forecasters say a few storms could become severe in coastal communities, especially within roughly 50 to 75 miles of the shoreline during the afternoon.

A marginal risk should be taken seriously without being exaggerated. Many locations may see ordinary rain or no severe weather at all, while a small number could experience a stronger cell. The exact placement of storms is uncertain, and a storm offshore may not affect the same communities as one that forms inland.

Coastal residents should monitor local NWS alerts, especially if traveling or working outdoors. If a thunderstorm approaches, go inside a substantial building, stay away from windows and postpone boating. Conditions should be reassessed as the front pushes offshore toward evening.
