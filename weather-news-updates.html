Wednesday Through the Weekend: Where the Weather Changes First

Good morning, Texas. The state is moving toward a wetter pattern, but the heaviest rainfall is not expected to be evenly distributed. Wednesday brings warmth, humidity and plenty of clouds for much of Central and North Texas. Isolated showers are possible in some areas, while West Texas begins preparing for a more organized weather system.

West Texas and the Permian Basin

The NWS Midland/Odessa forecast discussion identifies Thursday into Friday as the main window for numerous showers and thunderstorms, especially in the eastern half of its forecast area. Heavy rainfall may lead to flash flooding in urban areas, low-water crossings and other vulnerable locations. Early Wednesday fog may also briefly reduce visibility in parts of the Permian Basin and Lower Trans-Pecos.

South-Central Texas, the Hill Country and Rio Grande

From Austin and San Antonio to Marble Falls and Horseshoe Bay, expect a mild, humid Wednesday with considerable cloud cover. The more substantial late-week rain threat favors areas farther west, particularly Val Verde County and the southern Edwards Plateau. NWS Austin/San Antonio says isolated rainfall totals could reach four inches in favored locations, but much lower amounts are possible farther east. This is not a forecast of widespread flooding throughout the Hill Country.

Dallas–Fort Worth, Waco and North Texas

Above-normal temperatures continue into Thursday, with scattered rain becoming more plausible late Thursday through Saturday. NWS Fort Worth says the track of the approaching system has shifted north and west in recent forecasts, potentially limiting coverage east of Interstate 35. Many Halloween evening activities may avoid significant rain, although timing will need reassessment as the system evolves.

East Texas and the Gulf Coast

Rainfall placement remains uneven across the state. Residents of East Texas, Houston, Beaumont, Corpus Christi and the Coastal Bend should use their local NWS office for location-specific forecasts and any coastal alerts. The principal flash-flood concern highlighted in the forecast discussions reviewed for this report is farther west, not a blanket statewide emergency.

Tonight, Tomorrow and Early Next Week

Tonight stays humid in much of South-Central Texas. Thursday brings increasing rain coverage to western areas, with the most consequential downpours possible Thursday night and Friday. Conditions generally improve into the weekend, and forecasters are watching another possible cold front around Monday or Tuesday. Its timing and storm potential are not settled.

Previous Weather Report — October 27, 2026 (Archived Coverage)

TEXAS — Here's the big weather story today: Texas is a huge state, and the forecast isn't the same from El Paso to Houston. A localized severe-thunderstorm concern in Far West Texas deserves attention, while much of the rest of the state has a different set of day-to-day weather questions. The key is knowing which forecast applies to your community—not assuming a colorful statewide map means everyone faces the same threat.

Let's start in Far West Texas. The Storm Prediction Center's October 27 outlook identified a Marginal Risk for a small portion of the region. That's the lowest of the SPC's five severe-thunderstorm risk categories. It signals a limited, localized possibility of severe weather—not a prediction that widespread damaging storms will occur. For the exact area and timing, check the dated SPC outlook and local National Weather Service forecast.

What we're watching on radar

Take a look at the large map above. That display is observed live radar, which helps show precipitation being detected now. It is not a future forecast and it is not the HRRR model. When storms are moving, radar can help viewers follow the latest picture—but a warning from the National Weather Service always takes priority over a general radar display.

The 6 PM HRRR forecast: why the clock matters

Here's where weather models become useful. The High-Resolution Rapid Refresh, known as the HRRR, produces frequently updated short-range forecasts. Its simulated-reflectivity product can suggest where rain or thunderstorms might appear at 6 PM. For today, that's 6 PM CDT / 23:00 UTC. But the model run time and forecast hour must be verified together. We have not verified that specific frame, so this preview does not pretend the live radar is a 6 PM HRRR forecast.

Central Texas: a more comfortable afternoon

In a broad Texas forecast snapshot available for this afternoon, temperatures were around the upper 70s, with clouds mixing with some sunshine and a daytime high near 80 degrees. That's a useful general picture, but it is not a city-by-city observation. If you're in Austin, Marble Falls, San Antonio or the Hill Country, check the latest local NWS forecast before making outdoor plans.

Rainfall, temperatures and drought: four maps, four different stories

Now look at the panels to the right. The SPC map addresses severe-storm potential. The NWS temperature graphic depicts a forecast period, not necessarily the temperature outside your door right now. The Weather Prediction Center rainfall map shows projected accumulation over its labeled forecast period. And the U.S. Drought Monitor is a weekly assessment—not a live picture of rain falling today. Each panel can update after this article was published, so check the source timestamp.

What happens next?

Our focus through the next 24 to 72 hours is whether localized thunderstorm chances change, where the most meaningful rain opportunities develop, and whether any official watches or warnings are issued. We'll distinguish between what a model suggests and what forecasters have confirmed. That's the difference between a dramatic-looking weather graphic and information you can actually use.

Bottom line: Keep an eye on Far West Texas for the localized severe-weather concern, use the current radar for what's happening now, and rely on dated NWS and NOAA forecasts for what comes next. Texas News Studio will continue following the developing weather picture.
