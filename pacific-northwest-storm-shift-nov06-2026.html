NATIONAL WEATHER WATCH · NOVEMBER 6, 2026

STORMS SHIFT NORTHWEST!

Rain, mountain snow and possible flooding concerns move toward the Pacific Northwest.

A change in the western U.S. storm track is bringing relief to parts of the flood-weary Southwest while shifting attention toward the Pacific Northwest.

AccuWeather reported on November 4, with an update November 6, that portions of the Southwest were expected to see a break from major storms through mid-November as unsettled weather turned toward the Northwest.

For communities in Washington and Oregon, the combination of Pacific moisture, steep terrain and changing snow levels can create several different hazards: heavy rain in lower elevations, accumulating snow in the mountains, and runoff problems in vulnerable watersheds. The precise placement and severity of these impacts depend on evolving forecasts.

The story also illustrates how a shift in the jet stream can redistribute weather risks across the country. A quieter stretch in one region does not mean the broader pattern has become less active.

Residents and travelers should consult National Weather Service forecasts and local warnings before mountain travel or driving through flood-prone areas. This article is a national weather overview, not an active warning for a specific county.
